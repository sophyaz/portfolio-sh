OP_002 // ROCKETRY ● ACTIVE

Origami Recovery System

Recovery Systems lead for QMUL's National Rocketry Competition entry. Designing an origami-inspired parachute deployment mechanism — packing efficiency, controlled unfurl, descent stability.

AEROSPACE MECHANICAL CAD TEAM_LEAD
// PROJECT NOTES
Recovery systems on amateur rockets fail more often than you'd think. Tangled lines, late deployment, partial unfurl. The parachute is one of the simplest components in the whole vehicle and one of the hardest to get right under the constraints of a small airframe.

Manufacturing the system:​ We will be buying ripstop waterproof nylon to cut and shape the parachute out of, we will cut it to a 1m diameter. We will then attach kevlar cords via holes, burning the threads to secure it properly, our cords have a 2mm diameter. The parachute will be in the lower fuselage, motor ejection charge pressurises the lower fuselage which causes coupler separation and the parachute will be deployed. Nomex protection is used to protect the parachute from the ejection gases. ​

OP_001 // BCI MIDDLEWARE ● IN_DEV

Neural Command

Hardware-agnostic middleware translating human intent into mission commands for fleets of autonomous robots and drones. Targeting search-and-rescue, orbital robotics, and the training and coordination of robot fleets.

BCI PYTHON MNE ROBOTICS SAR ORBITAL
// LINKEDIN WRITEUP
Robot and drone fleets have a quiet bottleneck: the operator. Current control schemes: joysticks, mission-planning UIs, even voice, all force a human to translate intent into low-level commands one unit at a time. When you scale to ten, fifty, a hundred autonomous units, the operator becomes the slowest part of the loop. That's a real problem in search-and-rescue, where minutes decide outcomes, and in orbital robotics, where latency to ground-control already costs you.

Neural Command is my attempt at the inverse: a hardware-agnostic middleware layer that translates EEG-derived intent directly into fleet-level mission commands. Think "sweep this search grid", "converge on the heat signature", "regroup at base" issued at the speed of thought, executed autonomously by the fleet.

There's a second use case I'm increasingly excited about: training robots and drones. Demonstrating an intended behaviour through neural feedback is far closer to how humans actually teach each other than scripting reward functions or piloting a unit by hand. If middleware like this works, it changes how we onboard autonomous systems, not just how we command them.

Verticals on the radar: search-and-rescue, orbital robotics, robot training. If you work in any of those — or in BCI hardware — I'd love to talk.
OP_003 // HACKATHON ● SHIPPED

Let Em Cook

Receipt-scanning meal suggestion app. Built at hackathon using the Anthropic API, OCR pipeline parses grocery receipts, Claude generates contextual recipes from your actual pantry inventory.

CLAUDE_API OCR HACKATHON FULL_STACK
REPO →
// LINKEDIN WRITEUP
Built at a hackathon in just a day. The premise: most household food waste isn't from people being lazy, it's from a coordination problem. You've got ingredients in the fridge, but you can't think of what to make with what's actually there, so you order in instead, and the ingredients go off.

Let Em Cook is a single-page web app that closes that gap. You tell it what you've got — by typing, snapping a fridge photo, or pasting in a receipt and the Anthropic API generates contextual recipes from that exact inventory.

What I liked about this build: it reframes a behavioural problem as a UX one. You don't need to lecture people about waste; you just need to make the right action the easiest one.
